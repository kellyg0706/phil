## CL: the run-rate signal was right, and the shade under it was the error

The line of 0.96 sat at the TOP of the 0.89-0.96 analyst range. The consensus
was 0.926-0.929. On the playbook's at/above-consensus rule (lower consensus
plus one typical beat of 2-4c gives 0.95-0.97), that reads as market-agrees.
I recorded 0.28 against the 0.21 mid. The named upward signal in the note was
mechanical: Q1 and Q2 base EPS each grew 7-8% YoY, and 0.91 x 1.07 = 0.974. I
discounted it from my 0.32 shade view back toward the book. My reasons were
the FY guide implying H2 growth of about 3% and higher H2 ad spend.

The print was 0.97, exactly the H1 run-rate, and it cleared the line by one
cent. The estimate was directionally right (dB -0.106), and the outcome was
the closest possible call. This is the 9th at/above-consensus print within
about 5c of its line. It is also the first in the series where an above-book
deviation won, and the reason is that the note named a mechanical signal
rather than a beat count. That is consistent with the rule's
"named-signal deviations stay allowed" clause. It is the Yes-side mirror of
the TW/FCN "line beyond a mechanically implied range" signal (now 3-for-3
on the No side).

Was 0.28 too low? Given a 1c clearance, no. A run-rate centre of 0.974 with
a typical sd of ~2c puts P(>=0.97) near 0.5-0.6, but the FY-guide-implied H2
slowdown was real evidence pulling the other way, and it simply did not show
up in Q3. Variance on a coin-flip-sized margin, with the direction right.

Lesson (hypothesis, n=1): on a staples-type steady compounder, the H1 YoY
base-EPS growth applied to the prior-year quarter is a named mechanical
signal on the Yes side, just as an implied range is on the No side. Encoded
as a provisional note under the at/above-consensus rule, explicitly at n=1:
it permits a move off the book of the size the note carried (here +0.07),
and it does not support a bet until it reaches 3 graded cases.
