## Lessons

1. **Poll-mean error, not dispersion, on Brazil R1.** Two rows (22d7, 59fe)
   used a poll-mean margin with sd widened to 1.8 and then to 4.5. Both still
   put Lula first. The miss was a mean shift of about 6 points toward Flávio.
   Widening the sd moved the probability only a few points, so the correction
   was in the wrong place. Proposal for the next FULL retro: for a Brazil-style
   two-camp R1 read, carry the historical poll-to-outcome bias as a mean shift
   on the margin, and keep the sd as a separate term. One event, n=1, so no
   playbook edit yet.
2. **The veto held on 22d7 and the market was closer on 59fe.** Neither row
   is a veto success in the counterfactual sense: the vetoed side was the
   Lula-first side at 0.85, which lost. The veto's saving would need a Lula-first
   bet to count as realizable, and it is not clean evidence for or against the
   veto. Graded here, not appended to the frozen counterfactual table (DEEP-2026-09-30
   ruling).
3. **Renan 3rd is a method-gap row.** Forecast-only, no realizable counterfactual.
   Its only lesson is that small-candidate rank questions still need a graded
   base rate before a non-market edge is claimed.
