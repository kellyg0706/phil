## Finding 2 — a flagged-credibility poll was narrated but not actually discounted (Serbia)

`3d517af05bf6` (SNS/Vučić most seats) blended three polls: Faktor Plus
Sep16-20 (SNS 48.6/Students 37.1, **noted in the row's own rationale as
"pro-govt-accused pollster"**), NSPM early Sep (Students 44.4/SNS 41.7),
and CRTA June (Students 44.9/SNS 35.7). Two of three credible readings
had Students clearly ahead; the one favoring the incumbent carried a
self-flagged bias caveat. The forecast still landed on 0.68 for SNS
("incumbency/media advantage vs student momentum"), matching the market
— and Students List won. The caveat was written down but didn't move the
number: flagging a source's credibility problem in prose while still
averaging it in at full weight is not functionally different from
treating it as credible.

**Playbook edited** (`strategy/playbook.md`, new "Poll credibility
weighting" note under the polling-estimation section): when a source
carries a stated credibility problem (partisan ownership, government
pressure, accused bias), its weight in the blend must actually drop
toward zero, not just get a parenthetical caveat — if excluding it would
flip the direction of the blended estimate, that's the signal the caveat
was doing nothing. This is a single data point (n=1) so it does not
license a standing "weight incumbents down" directional prior — only the
method fix (make caveats load-bearing) is adopted here, mirroring how the
Rio-governor lesson was correctly scoped to method, not direction, before
it was over-generalized.
